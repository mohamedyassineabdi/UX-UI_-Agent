Methodology

Our structured 3-step approach to evaluating the user experience

1

Evidence Review

We isolate the homepage, core conversion pages, and the strongest commercial story signals before scoring.

2

Axis Review

The product is reviewed through the active UX/UI axes with rule-based evidence from the detailed audit.

3

Prioritization

Only the highest-impact friction points are kept, then converted into sales-facing recommendations.

Scoring

Five axes at a glance

1

Performance & Task Execution

How quickly and clearly the interface loads, responds, and helps a first-time visitor complete the primary task without friction.

6.6/10 Low severity
2

Flow & Architecture

How understandable the navigation model, page structure, and wayfinding feel across the visible journey.

5.8/10 Medium severity
3

Trust & Accessibility

How reliably the experience satisfies visible and runtime WCAG 2.2 accessibility expectations plus trust-critical usability signals.

2.3/10 High severity
4

Visual & UI Consistency

How consistently components, hierarchy, visual brand treatments, and recurring interaction patterns are applied across the visible experience.

3.9/10 High severity
5

Content & Microcopy

How clearly visible messaging, headings, labels, and CTA text explain value, meaning, and the next step.

5.8/10 Medium severity

Issues

Filter findings by priority or audit axis

01
Flow & Architecture Major Issue

Website layout breaks on phone screens

On Home, the audit found that the website does not adapt reliably from desktop to phone. Desktop viewport 1440x900; phone viewport 390x844. The phone render exposes desktop-layout content that does not adapt correctly, including `✕` (measured overflow: 0px).

Why it matters: This matters because mobile visitors cannot evaluate offers, navigation, or promotions with confidence when the page keeps desktop layout assumptions on a phone. In a context, this can directly reduce discovery, store/product engagement, and trust for first-time mobile users.

Recommended move: Rebuild the responsive breakpoint for the affected templates: remove fixed-width rows, let content groups collapse to a readable single-column flow, make hero/media blocks fluid, and test at 390px, 430px, 768px, and desktop widths before publishing.

Home Open page
02
Trust & Accessibility Major Issue

WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum): Text contrast is below the required readability threshold

On Contact, the rendered UI extractor found text whose measured foreground/background contrast is below the WCAG text contrast target. Examples include input#nom, input#societe, input#email.

Why it matters: These specific low-contrast labels and content blocks make Contact harder to read for low-vision users and in bright or low-quality display conditions. If the affected text is a form field, CTA, phone number, email, or article link, users can miss the action entirely.

Recommended move: Raise the foreground/background contrast for the cited text to at least 4.5:1 for normal text, or 3:1 for large text, then retest the affected components.

Contact Open page
03
Trust & Accessibility Major Issue

WCAG 2.2 SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value: Interactive controls could not be operated reliably

On Contact, Playwright attempted safe controls and found controls that produced no visible result, disappeared from the fresh page, or failed during activation. Examples: ✕: interaction threw an error.

Why it matters: When a cited control on Contact cannot be activated reliably, keyboard users, switch users, and ordinary mouse/touch users can all get blocked before completing the journey. This is materially different from a cosmetic issue because the intended action may be unreachable.

Recommended move: Fix the cited controls so activation works from a fresh page load, then retest with mouse and keyboard. If a control is hidden until a drawer opens, remove it from the tab/click path until it is actually visible.

Performance & Task Execution Major Issue

First Contentful Paint is slower than the visual feedback target

On Demander une démo, the audit measured fcp at 3799 ms, above the audit target of <= 1800 ms. This is based on the Playwright browser performance snapshot captured during the crawl.

Why it matters: On Demander une démo, this issue affects whether users can complete the intended action without hesitation or recovery work. The cited evidence should be fixed where it appears first, then retested on the same path.

Recommended move: Inline or preload critical CSS, defer non-critical JavaScript, and prioritize the first visible text or brand mark.

Demander une démo Open page
07
Performance & Task Execution Major Issue

Page load time is above the audit target

On Home, the audit measured load event at 9358 ms, above the audit target of <= 4000 ms. This is based on the Playwright browser performance snapshot captured during the crawl.

Why it matters: On Home, the page keeps loading far beyond the target window, which means users may see late assets, delayed scripts, or unstable readiness after the first screen appears. This affects perceived quality before users evaluate the content.

Recommended move: Reduce non-critical resources loaded during startup, lazy-load below-the-fold media, defer third-party scripts, and keep the initial route focused on the assets needed for the first screen.

08
Performance & Task Execution Major Issue

Page weight is heavy for a first visit

On Home, the audit measured transferred resources at 7.5 MB, above the audit target of <= 2.0 MB. This is based on the Playwright browser performance snapshot captured during the crawl.

Why it matters: On Home, the cited resource weight creates unnecessary waiting and bandwidth cost, especially on mobile or slower connections. Heavy startup assets also make performance regressions more likely as the site grows.

Recommended move: Compress and resize images, ship modern formats such as WebP/AVIF where possible, remove unused scripts/styles, and lazy-load media that is not visible in the first viewport.

Flow & Architecture Medium Issue

Page layouts are consistent across the whole website.

On Contact, the audit found that 'Page layouts are consistent across the whole website.' is not fully met. Some page-layout variation detected. Most audited pages share the same broad scaffold, but one or more pages deviate from the dominant layout structure.

Why it matters: On Contact, this issue affects how quickly users can understand where they are and where to go next. When the structure is unclear, users spend effort interpreting the site instead of evaluating the offer.

Recommended move: Standardize core page templates around the same header, content hierarchy, CTA, card/list, and footer structure, then document the exceptions that are intentionally page-specific.
