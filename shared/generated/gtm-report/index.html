Methodology

Our structured 3-step approach to evaluating the user experience

1

Evidence Review

We isolate the homepage, core conversion pages, and the strongest commercial story signals before scoring.

2

Axis Review

The product is reviewed through the active UX/UI axes with rule-based evidence from the detailed audit.

3

Prioritization

Only the highest-impact friction points are kept, then converted into sales-facing recommendations.

Scoring

Five axes at a glance

1

Performance & Task Execution

How quickly and clearly the interface loads, responds, and helps a first-time visitor complete the primary task without friction.

4.5/10 Medium severity
2

Flow & Architecture

How understandable the navigation model, page structure, and wayfinding feel across the visible journey.

3.5/10 High severity
3

Trust & Accessibility

How reliably the experience satisfies visible and runtime WCAG 2.2 accessibility expectations plus trust-critical usability signals.

4.3/10 High severity
4

Visual & UI Consistency

How consistently components, hierarchy, visual brand treatments, and recurring interaction patterns are applied across the visible experience.

5.5/10 Medium severity
5

Content & Microcopy

How clearly visible messaging, headings, labels, and CTA text explain value, meaning, and the next step.

4.2/10 High severity

Issues

Filter findings by priority or audit axis

01
Flow & Architecture Major Issue

Website layout breaks on phone screens

On Home, the audit found that the website does not adapt reliably from desktop to phone. Desktop viewport x; phone viewport x. The phone render exposes desktop-layout content that does not adapt correctly.

Why it matters: This matters because mobile visitors cannot evaluate offers, navigation, or promotions with confidence when the page keeps desktop layout assumptions on a phone. In a context, this can directly reduce discovery, store/product engagement, and trust for first-time mobile users.

Recommended move: Rebuild the responsive breakpoint for the affected templates: remove fixed-width rows, let content groups collapse to a readable single-column flow, make hero/media blocks fluid, and test at 390px, 430px, 768px, and desktop widths before publishing.

Flow & Architecture Major Issue

Lack of navigation structure

On Home, the AI review identified a issue that is not necessarily covered by the workbook criteria: No navigation menu or links to other pages are present on the page.

Why it matters: Without navigation, users cannot explore key sections (e.g., pricing, features) to evaluate the product, creating a dead end in the buyer journey.

Recommended move: Implement a clear navigation menu with key sections (e.g., Features, Pricing, Contact) to support user exploration.

Why it matters: This matters because the page feels slow, unresponsive, or harder to complete than it should. In a context, it can reduce clarity, trust, or sales readiness during a first review.

10
Trust & Accessibility Medium Issue

WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.7 Focus Visible: Focused controls do not show a reliable visible focus indicator

On Home, the keyboard probe reached controls whose computed focused state did not expose a strong outline or focus shadow.

Why it matters: Without a visible focus indicator on Home, keyboard users can lose track of where they are before activating a link, menu item, or form field.

Recommended move: Add a clear, high-contrast :focus-visible style to links, buttons, form fields, menu triggers, and custom controls.

Home
11
Trust & Accessibility Medium Issue

WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum): Interactive targets are smaller than WCAG 2.2 target-size expectations

On Home, several visible controls appear below the WCAG 2.2 target-size expectation. Examples include Learn more.

Why it matters: Small targets on Home increase mis-taps and make navigation harder for users with motor impairments, tremor, touch devices, or zoomed layouts. This is especially risky when the target is a navigation item, contact link, or primary CTA.

Recommended move: Increase the clickable area or spacing around the cited controls so each target has at least a 24 by 24 CSS-pixel target area unless a WCAG exception clearly applies.

Why it matters: On Home, the cited evidence creates a specific usability risk for this screen rather than a general design concern. Fixing it should make the affected action or content easier to understand and use.

Recommended move: Fix the cited WCAG 2.2 barrier, then retest the affected page with contrast checks, keyboard traversal, and control activation.
